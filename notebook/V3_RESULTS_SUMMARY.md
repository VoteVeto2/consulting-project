## 5. Renewal Action Matrix

### 5.1 Quadrant-Based Actions

| Quadrant | Risk Pattern | Recommended Action | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High freq / High sev** | Many costly claims | Strong repricing (+30-50 %), strict UW, possible non-renewal | **Immediate** |

### 5.2 Tier-Based Actions

| Risk Group | Share | Risk Index | Primary Action |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Very High (top 5 %) | 5 % | 2.4x | Strong repricing, strict UW, non-renewal review |
| High (80-95th pctl) | 15 % | 1.7x | Moderate repricing, deductible adjustment |
| Medium (50-80th) | 30 % | 1.1x | Monitor, selective repricing |
| Low (bottom 50 %) | 50 % | 0.6x | Retain, standard renewal |

### 5.3 Specific Recommendations

1. **Immediate repricing of the Very High tier** — 1,239 policies at 2.4x
   average expected loss. Current pricing almost certainly does not reflect
   this risk level. Non-renewal should be considered for the worst
   micro-segments.

2. **Deductible and coverage adjustment for High freq / Low sev** — these
   generate many small claims. Raising deductibles and steering to partner
   repair networks reduces claim costs without losing the customer.

3. **Underwriting review for Low freq / High sev** — rare but costly claims
   suggest coverage limits or car valuations may be inadequate. Retired
   policyholders are over-represented here (they claim rarely but expensively).

4. **Retain and protect the Low tier** — half the portfolio at below-average
   risk. Consider loyalty pricing to prevent adverse selection.

## 6. Key Findings & Caveats

### Headline results

1. **Risk is concentrated:** top 5 % of policies carry 12 % of expected loss
   at 2.4x the portfolio average. Gini = 0.296.

2. **GLM and GBM agree** on the risk ranking (Spearman = 0.88) — robust to
   model choice.

3. **Unprofitable profile is coherent:** young (~23 yr), male (~85 %),
   unemployed (~63 %), urban (~237 inh/km^2), no MD cover (~88 %).

4. **Frequency and severity tell different stories:**
   - Young unemployed -> high frequency AND high severity
   - Retired -> low frequency but high severity (rare, expensive claims)
   - CarType E -> increases frequency but *decreases* severity
   - This is why the quadrant decomposition matters for action design.

5. **Models are robust:**
   - Year-stable (AUC 0.685-0.687 across 2009/2010)
   - Gender-insensitive (AUC drops 0.003 without gender)
   - Distribution-insensitive (Gamma ~ Lognormal)
   - Common-cell calibration correlation = 0.91

### Honest caveats

1. **No premium data** — profitability is portfolio-relative, not absolute.
   Adding premium would enable loss-ratio analysis.
2. **Independence assumption** (freq independent of sev | x) is invoked, not
   tested. Without record-level linkage, residual dependence cannot be
   captured.
3. **~50 % claim rate** — likely balanced/sampled. Absolute probabilities
   need recalibration before production deployment.
4. **Severity tail** — GLMs underpredict extreme claims. GBM rank correlation
   (0.22) partially compensates; the ranking, not the point prediction, is
   what drives the profiling.
5. **Gender & EU law** — Test-Achats (2012) requires unisex pricing for new
   EU contracts. Gender used diagnostically only; gender-free model performs
   comparably and is recommended for compliant deployment.
